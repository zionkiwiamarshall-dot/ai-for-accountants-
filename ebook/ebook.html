AI foundations for finance work

What AI tools actually are, where they help accountants most, and the one rule that keeps you safe: AI drafts, you verify.
In this chapter you will learn to
  • Explain how AI chat tools work
  • Name 5 strong and 5 weak uses in finance
  • Apply the draft-and-verify rule

Lesson 1.1What an AI chat tool really is

Tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot are built on large language models. A language model has read a huge amount of text and learned to predict what words come next. That is why it writes fluent emails, explains IFRS concepts clearly and turns messy notes into a tidy memo.

It is also why it can be confidently wrong. The model is producing likely-sounding text, not looking facts up in a ledger. Unless the tool is connected to your data or to the web, it does not know your client's balances, this year's tax rates or what your auditor asked for last week.

Three things every accountant should know

  1. It is a language engine, not a calculator. Plain chat answers can get arithmetic wrong, especially with long lists of numbers. Some tools can run real calculations (for example ChatGPT's data analysis, or Copilot inside Excel), but you still check the result.
  2. It does not know what it does not know. Ask about a tax rule it is unsure of and it may invent a plausible rate or section number. Accountants call this a fabricated source. AI people call it a hallucination.
  3. Output quality follows input quality. A vague request gets a generic answer. A request with context, your format and your checks gets work you can use.
Tip

Think of AI as a very fast, well-read junior staff member on their first day. Brilliant at drafting, structuring and explaining. Not yet trusted to sign anything off.

Lesson 1.2Where AI helps accountants, and where it doesn't

The best uses are tasks that are heavy on words, structure or logic and where you can easily check the result. The worst uses are tasks where the AI would be the only source of truth.

Strong usesWeak or risky uses
Writing and explaining Excel formulasAdding up a column of 300 numbers in chat
Drafting variance commentary from numbers you provideDeciding the correct tax treatment on its own
Turning a messy process into a step-by-step checklistProducing final figures for a report without a check
Summarising a 40-page contract for lease or revenue termsQuoting the "current" tax rate without a primary source
Explaining a financial statement to a non-finance managerPasting identifiable client or payroll data into a public tool
Drafting policy, procedure and audit-request documentsSigning off audit evidence or professional judgements
Illustrative example

A finance officer at a mid-sized NGO spends two days each quarter writing narrative for the donor's budget-versus-actual report. She now pastes the variance table (no staff names) into an AI tool with a clear prompt, gets a first draft in two minutes, then spends one hour correcting and adding context only she knows. Two days becomes half a day.

The real time savings

Most accountants do not save time on the calculations themselves. They save it on the work around the numbers: writing explanations, building checklists, finding the right formula, reading long documents and replying to questions. That is where to start.

Lesson 1.3The golden rule: AI drafts, you verify

Professional responsibility does not move to the software. If an AI-drafted note goes into a report with your name on it, the note is yours. So every AI output in finance goes through a quick verification step before it is used.

The 4-check routine

  1. Numbers: Does every figure match the source? Recalculate totals in your spreadsheet, never in chat.
  2. Facts and rules: Is every rate, standard or legal reference confirmed from a primary source (the standard, the law, or the tax authority's or regulator's official website)?
  3. Logic: Does the explanation actually follow from the numbers? AI sometimes gives a reason that sounds right but is not supported.
  4. Fit: Is the tone, format and level of detail right for the reader?
Watch out

Be most careful when an answer is exactly what you hoped to hear. AI tools tend to agree with the way a question is framed. Ask "what could be wrong with this?" as a follow-up.

Ask the AI to check itself
Review the answer you just gave me as a sceptical senior auditor would.
1. List every number, rate or rule you stated, and say how confident you are in each (high / medium / low).
2. Point out any assumption you made that I did not give you.
3. Tell me what I should verify against a primary source before using this.

This does not replace your own check. It makes your check faster by showing you where the weak spots are.

Apply it at work

Map your own week

Find the three tasks in your real work where AI would save you the most time, safely.

  1. List 10 tasks you did in the last two weeks. Include small ones like replying to queries or fixing formulas.
  2. Paste the list (no client names or figures) into an AI tool with the starter prompt below.
  3. Challenge the result. Move anything that would make the AI the only source of truth to the risky column.
  4. Pick the top three tasks to try during this course and write them in your notes.

Confidentiality, data protection and professional ethics

What you must never paste into an AI tool, how to anonymise data in two minutes, and how to use AI in line with your professional code.
In this chapter you will learn to
  • Classify data as safe, anonymise or never
  • Anonymise a dataset before using AI
  • Document AI use for review

Lesson 2.1Green, amber, red: what you can share

Accountants handle some of the most sensitive data in any organisation: salaries, bank details, tax IDs, client finances. Before you use any AI tool, decide which category your data falls into.

CategoryExamplesRule
Green: safePublic standards, generic questions, formula help with made-up data, your own drafted text with no client detailsUse any approved tool
Amber: anonymise firstTrial balances, variance tables, transaction lists, contract termsRemove names and identifiers, round or scale figures if needed, then use an approved tool
Red: never in a public toolPayroll with names, bank account numbers, national ID and TIN numbers, passwords, unreleased results of listed companies, anything under a confidentiality agreementOnly in an enterprise tool your organisation has approved for that data, or not at all
Watch out

Free consumer versions of AI tools may use your conversations to improve their models unless you switch this off. Enterprise and business plans usually do not, and offer stronger contracts. Check your tool's settings and your organisation's policy before you start.

The law applies too

Most countries now have data protection laws, such as the GDPR in the EU and UK and national data protection acts across Africa, Asia and the Americas. They set rules for how personal data is collected, used and transferred, including to other countries. Sending personal data to an AI service hosted abroad can count as a cross-border transfer. When in doubt, anonymise or ask your data protection officer.

Lesson 2.2Anonymise in two minutes

Most finance tasks do not need real names. The AI needs the shape of the data, not the identity of the people in it.

A quick anonymising routine

  1. Replace names with codes. "Bright Star Traders Ltd" becomes "Supplier A". Keep your key in a separate sheet that never leaves your computer.
  2. Delete identifiers. Remove TINs, account numbers, phone numbers, emails and addresses.
  3. Scale if needed. If the size of the figures is itself sensitive, multiply everything by the same factor (for example 0.37). Ratios and trends stay correct.
  4. Strip hidden data. Copy values into a new sheet rather than uploading the original file, so comments, hidden tabs and file properties do not go with it.
Illustrative example

Before: "Salary advance to Jane Doe, staff ID 0457, 1,200,000, Account 01J2045..."
After: "Salary advance to Employee 07, 1,200,000"

Anonymise a list with AI (made-up data only)
I will paste a sample of column headings and 3 rows of FAKE data.
Write me step-by-step Excel instructions (or a formula) to:
1. replace every name in column B with a code like "Party 001"
2. create a separate key table mapping codes to names
3. remove columns that contain IDs, phone numbers or account numbers
I will run this on the real data myself, on my own computer.

[paste headings and 3 fake rows]
Tip

This pattern is useful throughout the course: show the AI fake or sample data, get the method, then apply the method yourself to the real data.

Lesson 2.3Your professional code still applies

Professional bodies such as IFAC, ACCA, ICAEW and national accounting boards expect members to follow the fundamental principles of integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality and professional behaviour. AI does not change these principles. It changes how you apply them.

PrincipleWhat it means with AI
IntegrityDo not present AI output as independent analysis you did not do or check.
ObjectivityBe aware the AI may lean towards the answer your question implies. Ask for the opposing view.
Competence and due careUnderstand the limits of the tool and verify its output before use.
ConfidentialityApply the green, amber, red rule every time.
Professional behaviourFollow your firm's AI policy and be open about AI use when it matters.

Keep a light AI log

For anything that goes to a client, auditor, board or regulator, keep a short record: what tool you used, what for, and what you checked. A simple column in your working papers is enough.

Illustrative example

AI use note: First draft of variance commentary produced with [tool] from anonymised table. All figures checked to TB on [date]. Explanations for lines 4 and 9 rewritten from management discussion. Reviewed by [name].

Apply it at work

Anonymise a real working file

Take a real (amber) file you use often and make an AI-safe copy of it.

  1. Choose a file you use often, such as a transaction export, aged debtors list or variance table.
  2. Copy the values into a new workbook. Do not upload or paste the original.
  3. Apply the routine: codes for names, delete identifiers, scale if needed. Keep the key separate.
  4. Use the starter prompt with your anonymised copy to get a short summary, and confirm nothing identifying remains.

Prompting for finance-grade output

A simple 5-part prompt structure that turns generic AI answers into work you can actually use, plus how to iterate when the first answer misses.
In this chapter you will learn to
  • Write prompts with the RCTFC structure
  • Iterate on weak answers
  • Build reusable prompt templates

Lesson 3.1The RCTFC structure

Most poor AI answers come from short, vague prompts. Use five parts. You do not need all five every time, but the more you include, the better the answer.

PartQuestion it answersExample
RoleWho should the AI act as?"Act as a senior management accountant…"
ContextWhat is the situation?"…for a mid-sized NGO reporting quarterly to two donors…"
TaskWhat exactly should it do?"…draft commentary on the five largest variances below…"
FormatWhat should the output look like?"…one short paragraph each, plain English, under 80 words…"
ChecksWhat limits and checks apply?"…use only the figures I give you; if a reason is not clear, write [CONFIRM WITH MANAGER] instead of guessing."
Illustrative example

Weak: "Explain these variances."
Strong: the full prompt below.

Variance commentary (RCTFC)
Role: Act as a senior management accountant.
Context: I work for a mid-sized organisation. Below is our budget vs actual for Q3 in [currency]. Notes from the budget holders are in the last column.
Task: Draft commentary for the five largest variances by absolute value.
Format: One paragraph per variance, plain English, under 80 words each, starting with the line name in bold.
Checks: Use only the figures and notes I provide. Do not invent reasons. If the cause is unclear, write [CONFIRM WITH BUDGET HOLDER]. Do not recalculate totals.

[paste table]
Tip

The Checks part is what makes prompts finance-grade. Telling the AI exactly what to do when it does not know stops most invented answers.

Lesson 3.2Iterate: the second prompt matters most

Treat the first answer as a first draft. Experienced users rarely accept it as-is. Instead they give short, specific follow-ups.

Useful follow-ups

  • Narrow it: "Shorter. Cut each paragraph to two sentences."
  • Change the reader: "Rewrite for a board member with no finance background."
  • Show the working: "Show the steps you used, so I can check them."
  • Challenge it: "What is the strongest argument that this conclusion is wrong?"
  • Fix one thing: "Keep everything except point 3, which is wrong because [reason]."
Make the AI ask before answering
Before you answer, ask me up to 5 questions about anything you would need to know to do this well. Wait for my answers, then complete the task.

Task: [describe your task]

This is one of the most useful prompts in the course. It is especially good for policies, procedures and reports, where the AI would otherwise fill gaps with generic assumptions.

Watch out

Long conversations drift. If a chat has gone on for many turns and the answers are getting worse, start a new chat and paste in a clean, complete prompt.

Lesson 3.3Build your own prompt templates

Once a prompt works, save it. A personal library of 10 to 15 tested prompts covers most of an accountant's repeated work. Use square brackets for the parts you change each time.

Template: explain a financial concept
Explain [concept, e.g. deferred income] to [reader, e.g. a programme manager with no finance background].
Use one simple example with round numbers from [our sector, e.g. a grant received in advance].
Keep it under 150 words. End with one sentence on why it matters to them.
Template: turn notes into a procedure
Turn my rough notes below into a clear step-by-step procedure for [process, e.g. petty cash replenishment].
Format: numbered steps, who does each step, and the evidence kept at each step.
Add a short "controls" section listing where errors or fraud could happen and the check that prevents it.
Ask me if any step is unclear rather than guessing.

[paste notes]
Template: review my draft
Review the draft below as a [reviewer, e.g. finance director].
List: (1) anything unclear, (2) anything that could be misread, (3) any claim not supported by the figures, (4) suggested wording fixes.
Do not rewrite the whole thing.

[paste draft]
Tip

Every prompt in this course is collected in the Prompt library page of this site. Copy them, adapt them and keep your own versions in a document your team can share.

Apply it at work

Rebuild a weak prompt

Take a task from your top three (Module 1) and turn it into a tested, reusable RCTFC prompt.

  1. Write the short, lazy version of the prompt you would normally type, and run it.
  2. Rewrite it with all five RCTFC parts and run it again on the same anonymised input.
  3. Use at least two follow-ups to improve the answer.
  4. Save the final prompt as a template with [square brackets] for the parts you change.

Excel and spreadsheets with AI

Get the right formula first time, understand inherited spreadsheets, clean messy exports and automate repeated steps.
In this chapter you will learn to
  • Generate and test formulas
  • Decode unfamiliar spreadsheets
  • Clean bank and system exports

Lesson 4.1Writing formulas you can trust

This is the quickest win for most accountants. Describe the result you want in plain words, name your columns, and say which version of Excel or Google Sheets you use. Newer functions such as XLOOKUP, FILTER and LET are not available in older Excel versions.

Get a formula
I use [Excel 365 / Excel 2016 / Google Sheets].
Sheet "Transactions" has: A = Date, B = Account code, C = Description, D = Amount, E = Cost centre.
Sheet "Summary" has account codes in column A and months (as real dates, first of month) in row 1, from B1.
Write a formula for cell B2 that totals Amount for that account code and that month, so I can copy it across and down.
Explain each part of the formula in one line.

Always test the formula

  1. Pick two or three cells and check them by hand with a filter or a pivot table.
  2. Test an edge case: a month with no transactions, a negative amount, a code that does not exist.
  3. Check what happens when someone adds rows. Does the range still cover them?
Watch out

A formula can be correct and still be wrong for your data, for example if dates are stored as text or codes have trailing spaces. If results look odd, ask the AI: "What data problems could make this formula return the wrong result?"

Lesson 4.2Understanding a spreadsheet you inherited

Every finance team has a workbook nobody fully understands. AI is very good at explaining formulas line by line.

Explain a formula
Explain this Excel formula step by step in plain English, as if to a new accountant.
Then tell me: what could make it return a wrong answer, and how would you simplify it?

=IFERROR(INDEX(Rates!$C:$C,MATCH(1,(Rates!$A:$A=B5)*(Rates!$B:$B<=C5),0)),0)

For a whole model, list the sheets, what each one seems to do and a few key formulas. Ask the AI to map how the numbers flow from input sheets to the final report. You get a short "how this workbook works" note that the next person will thank you for.

Document a workbook
Below is a list of sheets in a workbook and the key formulas on each.
Write a one-page guide: purpose of each sheet, how data flows between them (inputs → calculations → outputs), which cells a user should change each month, and the 3 biggest risks of error.

[paste sheet names and key formulas]

Lesson 4.3Cleaning exports and automating repeats

Bank statements, mobile money exports and system reports rarely arrive clean. Typical problems are dates as text, amounts with commas or "Dr/Cr" labels, merged headers and blank rows. AI can write the steps to fix them.

Clean a messy export
Here are the headers and 5 sample rows (fake values) from a bank statement export.
Problems: dates are text like "03/07/2026", amounts have commas, debits and credits are in one column with "DR" or "CR" at the end, and there are blank rows between pages.
Give me step-by-step instructions using [Power Query / formulas] to produce a clean table with: Date (real date), Description, Debit, Credit, Net amount.

[paste headers and sample rows]
Tip

Power Query (Data → Get & Transform in Excel) records your cleaning steps, so next month you just click Refresh. Ask the AI to write the steps for Power Query rather than one-off formulas when the task repeats.

Macros and scripts

AI can also write VBA macros (Excel) or Apps Script (Google Sheets). These are powerful, but a macro can delete or overwrite data. Rules:

  • Always run a new macro on a copy of the file first.
  • Ask the AI to add comments explaining each line, and read them.
  • Never run a macro that sends emails or touches files outside the workbook until you understand every line.
Illustrative example

Using Copilot in Excel or Gemini in Sheets, you can ask directly: "Highlight duplicate invoice numbers in column C" or "Add a column showing days overdue from today". The same testing rules apply.

Apply it at work

Fix one real spreadsheet problem

Use AI to solve a formula, cleaning or documentation problem you actually have.

  1. Choose a real problem: a formula you need, a workbook you do not understand, or a messy export.
  2. Describe it to the AI using fake sample data that has the same structure.
  3. Apply the answer to your real file on your own computer.
  4. Test it: check 3 cells by hand and try one edge case.

Lesson 5.1Coding transactions consistently

Inconsistent coding causes most month-end headaches. Instead of asking AI to code every line (risky and slow to check), ask it to help you write clear coding rules that you and your team apply, or that your accounting software can use as bank rules.

Draft coding rules
Here is our chart of accounts (codes and names) and 30 anonymised sample bank descriptions with the account they were coded to.
1. Write a set of coding rules, for example "descriptions containing X go to account Y".
2. Flag any samples that look inconsistently coded.
3. List descriptions that are too vague to code by rule and need a human decision.
Present the rules as a table I can turn into bank rules.

[paste chart of accounts]
[paste samples]
Tip

Mobile money and bank descriptions are often cryptic. Ask the AI to group similar descriptions first ("cluster these by likely payee type"). The groups make rule writing much faster.

Watch out

AI suggestions for coding are suggestions. VAT treatment, capital vs revenue and donor-restricted vs unrestricted decisions need a human with the full facts.

Lesson 5.2Investigating reconciliation exceptions

The matching itself is best done in your accounting software or spreadsheet. AI helps with the part that takes thinking: explaining the leftovers.

Explain unreconciled items
Act as an experienced accountant reviewing a bank reconciliation.
Below are the unmatched items from the bank side and the ledger side (anonymised).
For each item, suggest the 2 most likely explanations (timing difference, duplicate, bank charge, error in amount, wrong account, etc.), and what evidence I should look for to confirm.
Also point out any pairs that might match each other despite small differences in amount or date.
Do not claim anything is confirmed.

Ledger unmatched: [paste]
Illustrative example

The AI noticed that a ledger receipt of 1,250,000 and a bank credit of 1,248,500 three days later probably match, with a 1,500 transfer fee. The accountant confirmed it from the bank advice and posted the fee. Ten minutes instead of an hour of searching.

Common patterns AI is good at spotting

  • Transposed digits (54,310 vs 53,410), where the difference divides by 9
  • The same amount posted twice in the ledger
  • Round-sum receipts split across several bank lines
  • Recurring bank charges never posted

Lesson 5.3A tighter month-end close

A clear, owned checklist is the single best tool for a faster close. AI can turn your team's scattered knowledge into one.

Build a close checklist
Help me build a month-end close checklist for [type of organisation, e.g. an NGO with 3 donors and 40 staff].
Our systems: [e.g. QuickBooks, Excel payroll, two bank accounts and a mobile money account].
Organise it by day (Day -2 to Day +5), with: task, owner role, evidence produced, and the control it supports.
Include accruals, prepayments, payroll, bank and mobile money reconciliations, intercompany or fund transfers, fixed assets, and donor fund balances.
Ask me questions first if you need more detail.
Accruals prompt
Based on this list of recurring monthly costs and the invoices received so far this month, list the expenses that probably need accruing, with a suggested estimate method for each (last month, contract value, usage).
Mark which ones I must confirm with the budget holder.

Invoices received: [paste]
Tip

After each close, spend five minutes asking the AI: "Here are the problems we had this month. Suggest changes to the checklist to prevent them next month." Over a few months the checklist becomes very strong.

Apply it at work

Draft your close checklist

Analysis, reporting and management packs

Turn numbers into clear stories for managers, boards and donors: variance analysis, ratio explanations and report drafting.
In this chapter you will learn to
  • Draft variance and trend commentary
  • Explain ratios to non-finance readers
  • Structure a management or donor report

Lesson 6.1From numbers to insight

Managers rarely want more numbers. They want to know what changed, why, and what to do. AI is a strong first-draft writer for this, as long as you give it the numbers and the known reasons.

The "so what" structure

  1. What happened: the movement, in figures and %
  2. Why: the cause, from budget holders or your analysis
  3. So what: the impact on the year, cash or targets
  4. Now what: the action or decision needed
Trend analysis
Below is monthly revenue and cost data for 12 months (scaled).
1. Describe the 3 most important trends in plain English.
2. Point out any month that looks unusual and suggest possible reasons I should check.
3. Suggest 2 charts that would show this best to a management team.
Use only the data given. Do not calculate new totals; I will do that in Excel.

[paste data]
Tip

Calculate percentages and totals in your spreadsheet first and paste them in. Then the AI only writes, and you never need to check its arithmetic.

Lesson 6.2Explaining ratios and statements

Much of an accountant's value is translation. AI makes it easier to explain finance in the reader's language.

Explain to a non-finance reader
Explain our current ratio of 0.8 and debtor days of 74 to a board of a [type of organisation] with no finance background.
Use one everyday comparison. Say why each matters for us, what "good" usually looks like in our sector (and say this varies), and one question the board should ask management.
Under 200 words.
Statement walk-through
Write a one-page "how to read our financial statements" guide for new programme managers.
Cover: statement of financial position, statement of income and expenditure, and cash flow, in simple terms.
Use examples from an organisation that receives restricted donor grants.
End with 5 questions a manager should ask every quarter.
Watch out

"Good" benchmarks vary a lot by sector and country. If the AI gives a benchmark, treat it as a starting point and confirm from a reliable industry source or your own history.

Lesson 6.3Drafting management and donor reports

Use AI to build the structure and the first draft. Keep the figures, judgements and final wording with you.

Management pack outline
Create an outline for a monthly management accounts pack for [organisation].
Audience: [CEO and department heads]. Length: max 6 pages.
Include: a one-page summary with 5 key messages, KPIs, income and expenditure vs budget, cash and forecast, risks, and decisions needed.
For each section, say what figures I need to supply and what the commentary should answer.
Donor financial narrative
Draft the financial narrative section of a donor report.
Grant period: [dates]. Budget: [total]. Spent to date: [amount, %].
Main variances and reasons: [paste].
Tone: factual, transparent, no excuses. Explain underspends with a realistic plan to use the funds, and overspends with how they are being managed.
Mark anything that needs donor approval (such as budget realignment) with [REQUIRES DONOR APPROVAL].
Max 400 words.
Illustrative example

A good final step: paste your finished draft back in and ask, "Which sentences could a donor misread, or read as hiding a problem?" Fix those before you send it.

Apply it at work

Write this month's commentary

Produce real commentary for a report you already prepare, in less than half the usual time.

  1. Prepare an anonymised or scaled variance table, with percentages calculated in Excel and known reasons noted.
  2. Use the starter prompt to get a first draft using the what, why, so what, now what structure.
  3. Rewrite anything that is wrong, vague or missing context.
  4. Ask the AI which sentences a reader could misread, and fix them.

Tax, compliance and audit preparation

Use AI to research faster, prepare better for audits and document controls, while every rule and rate is confirmed from a primary source.
In this chapter you will learn to
  • Research tax questions safely
  • Prepare audit request lists and responses
  • Document internal controls

Lesson 7.1Tax research: a starting point, never the answer

Tax rules change often, differ by country and depend on facts. AI is useful for framing the question, listing what to check and explaining concepts. It is not a reliable source for current rates, thresholds or deadlines.

Safe tax research routine

  1. Use AI to understand the concept and list the questions you need answered.
  2. Go to the primary source: the Act and regulations, the tax authority's official website, official guidance and annual finance laws.
  3. For anything material or unusual, get advice from a qualified tax adviser.
  4. Document your sources in your working papers.
Frame a tax question
I need to work out the tax treatment of [describe transaction, no client names] in [country].
Do not give me a final answer or quote rates.
Instead:
1. Explain the main tax concepts involved.
2. List the specific questions I need to answer from the law and official guidance.
3. List the facts I need to gather from the client.
4. Tell me which kinds of primary sources usually cover this.
Watch out

If an AI tool quotes a rate, deadline or section number, treat it as unconfirmed until you have seen it in the official source. Finance acts change rates every year.

Lesson 7.3Internal controls and policies

Many organisations have controls that work but are not written down, which auditors and donors flag. AI is excellent at turning practice into documentation.

Document a control
Turn this description of how we approve payments into a formal control description.
Include: control objective, risk addressed, control activity, frequency, owner, evidence, and how it is reviewed.
Then list 3 weaknesses you can see and suggest improvements.

How we do it now: [describe]
Draft a policy
Draft a [policy, e.g. procurement / travel and per diem / asset management / AI use] policy for a [type and size] organisation.
Sections: purpose, scope, roles, procedure, approval limits (leave the limits as [AMOUNT] for me to fill), documentation, exceptions, review date.
Plain English, max 2 pages. Ask me 5 questions first.

Using AI to spot risk

Give the AI an anonymised list (for example vendor master data or a payments list) and ask for patterns worth investigating: duplicate bank details across suppliers, payments just below approval limits, round amounts, weekend postings. Treat these as leads for investigation, not findings.

Watch out

Never put an accusation in writing based only on an AI-spotted pattern. Every pattern needs evidence and a proper process.

Apply it at work

  2. Pick one high-risk area (such as grants, payroll or fixed assets).
  3. Ask the AI to act as the auditor and list questions for that area.
  4. Note which questions you cannot yet answer with evidence.
